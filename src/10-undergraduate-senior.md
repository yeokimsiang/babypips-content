# Undergraduate: Senior {undergraduate-senior}

## Risk Management {risk-management}

### Risk Management Explained {risk-management-explained}
- Protecting capital comes first; survival lets an edge play out.

### Drawdown Explained {drawdown-explained}
- Peak-to-trough decline. Recovery needed = 1/(1 − drawdown) − 1: a 50% loss needs a 100% gain.

### How Much Should You Risk Per Trade? {how-much-should-you-risk-per-trade}
- Common guideline: 1% to 2% of equity per trade, less while learning.

### Risk Budgeting {risk-budgeting}
- Cap total risk per day, week and across open trades.

### Position Sizing: Calculating Trade Size {position-sizing-how-to-calculate-your-trade-size}
- Size = (account × risk %) / (stop in pips × pip value per lot).

### Reward to Risk Ratio and Expectancy {reward-to-risk-ratio-and-expectancy}
- Breakeven win rate = 1 / (1 + R:R). Positive expectancy needs the right mix of win rate and R:R.

### Your Personal Risk Management Plan {building-your-personal-risk-management-plan}
- Write down risk per trade, daily stop, max open risk and rules after losing streaks.

## The Number 1 Cause of Death of Forex Traders {the-number-1-cause-of-death-of-forex-traders}

### Why Leverage Kills Most New Traders {why-leverage-kills-most-new-traders}
- Over-leverage turns ordinary moves into account ending losses.

### Leverage and Margin {leverage-and-margin}
- Leverage is a tool; margin is the deposit. More leverage means less room for error.

### Global Leverage Landscape {global-leverage-landscape}
- Regulators cap retail leverage (e.g. 30:1 majors in the EU, UK and Australia, 50:1 in the US); offshore brokers offer far more.

### The Margin Call {the-margin-call}
- When equity falls below required margin, positions get closed at the worst time.

### Effective vs Offered Leverage {effective-leverage-vs-offered-leverage}
- Effective leverage = total position notional / equity. That, not the broker's maximum, is your real risk.

### Six Leverage Myths {six-leverage-myths-that-destroy-accounts}
- Myths like "high leverage means high profits" or "small accounts need high leverage".

### Prop Firms and Funded Accounts {prop-firms-and-funded-accounts}
- Drawdown limits at prop firms force leverage discipline.

### Using Leverage Without Blowing Up {how-to-use-leverage-without-blowing-your-account}
- Size by risk, not by available margin; keep effective leverage low.

### Portfolio Heat {portfolio-heat-managing-total-risk-exposure}

## Setting Stop Losses {setting-stop-losses}

### What is a Stop Loss? {what-is-a-stop-loss}
- A pre-set exit that defines the most you will lose on a trade.

### Stop Placement {stop-placement}
- Place where your trade idea is proven wrong, then size the position to fit.

### How to Set a Stop Loss {how-to-set-stop-loss}
- Methods: percentage, chart based (beyond swing or level), volatility based, and time stops.

### Setting Stops Using Volatility {how-to-set-stop-using-volatility}
- Stop at a multiple of ATR (e.g. 1.5 to 2 × ATR) to stay outside normal noise.

### Why Your Stop Keeps Getting Hit {why-your-stop-keeps-getting-hit}
- Stops too tight, at obvious levels, or ignoring spread and volatility.

### Stop Loss Mistakes {stop-loss-mistakes}
- Moving stops further away, not using them, and placing them on round numbers.

### Stop Loss Playbook {stop-loss-playbook}
- Pick the method by setup and market environment.

## Scaling In and Out {scaling-in-and-out}

### Scaling {scaling}
- Adding to or reducing a position in stages rather than all at once.

### Scaling Out of a Winner {how-to-scale-out-a-winning-position}
- Take partial profits at targets and trail the rest; trades off maximum gain for consistency.

### Pyramiding {pyramiding}
- Add to winners as the trend confirms, with smaller additions and stops moved up.

### ATR Based Sizing for Scaling {atr-based-sizing-for-scale-in-and-scale-out}
- Space entries and exits by ATR multiples.

### Scaling Into a Zone {scaling-into-a-zone}
- Split entries across a support or resistance zone; total risk still fixed in advance.

### Scaling Playbook {scaling-playbook}
- Rules for when to add, when to reduce, and how to keep total risk capped.

## Currency Correlations {currency-correlations}

### Currency Correlation Explained {currency-correlation-explained}
- Coefficient from −1 to +1 measuring how two pairs move together.

### How to Read Correlation {how-to-read-correlation}
- Above +0.7 strong positive, below −0.7 strong negative, near zero unrelated.

### Pairs That Move Together {currency-pairs-that-move-together}
- EUR/USD and GBP/USD positive; EUR/USD and USD/CHF negative.

### Commodity Currencies and Hidden Drivers {commodity-currencies-and-their-hidden-drivers}
- AUD, NZD, CAD share drivers in commodities and risk mood.

### Cross-Asset Correlations {beyond-fx-cross-asset-correlations}
- Links between FX and gold, oil, yields and equities.

### Correlations Change {correlations-change}
- Correlations shift with regimes; check them regularly.

### Are You Doubling Your Risk? {are-you-doubling-your-risk}
- Two trades in highly correlated pairs act like one bigger position.

### Knowing Your True Exposure {knowing-your-true-exposure}
- Net out exposure by currency, e.g. long EUR/USD plus short USD/CHF is double short USD.

### Using Correlations to Avoid Bad Trades {using-correlations-to-avoid-bad-trades}
- Use related pairs to confirm or question a setup.

### When Correlations Break {when-correlations-break}
- Breaks often signal a currency specific story or regime shift.

### How to Calculate Correlations {how-to-calculate-correlations}
- Pearson correlation on returns over a chosen window, e.g. with a spreadsheet CORREL function.
