# Undergraduate: Freshman {undergraduate-freshman}

## Fundamental Analysis {fundamental-analysis}

### The Economic Cycle {the-economic-cycle-where-every-fundamental-analysis-starts}
- Expansion, peak, contraction, trough. Where an economy sits in the cycle frames rate expectations and currency direction.

### Economic Growth {economic-growth}
- GDP growth attracts investment and supports the currency; slowing growth points to easier policy.

### Inflation {inflation}
- Rising inflation pushes central banks to hike, usually bullish for the currency unless it signals stagflation.

### Monetary Policy: What Central Banks Do {monetary-policy-what-central-banks-do}
- Set policy rates, run QE or QT, and guide expectations to meet inflation and employment goals.

### Hawkish vs Dovish {hawkish-vs-dovish-how-read-central-bank-language}
- Hawkish = leaning to tighten (currency positive); dovish = leaning to ease (currency negative). Changes in tone matter most.

### Interest Rates: The Price of Money {interest-rates-the-price-of-money}
- Capital flows toward higher and rising real yields; rate differentials drive FX.

### Central Banks Behind the Major Currencies {central-banks-behind-major-currencies}
- Fed (USD), ECB (EUR), BoE (GBP), BoJ (JPY), SNB (CHF), BoC (CAD), RBA (AUD), RBNZ (NZD) and their mandates.

### Central Bank Personalities {central-bank-personalities}
- Each bank and governor has tendencies (hawk, dove, intervention prone) worth knowing.

### Trading Central Bank Decisions {trading-central-bank-decisions}
- Compare the decision, statement and projections with expectations; surprises move price.

### How Fiscal Policy Moves Currencies {how-fiscal-policy-moves-currencies}
- Government spending and taxes affect growth, debt and yields, and so the currency.

## Country Risk and Currency Drivers {country-risk-and-currency-drivers}

### Danger Premium {danger-premium}

### Currency Intervention {currency-intervention-when-central-banks-enter-the-market}

## Currency Crosses {currency-crosses}

### What is a Currency Cross Pair? {what-is-a-currency-cross-pair}
- A pair without USD, e.g. EUR/JPY, GBP/AUD; its rate is derived from the two USD pairs.

### The Hidden Force Moving Dollar Pairs {hidden-force-moving-dollar-pairs}
- USD pairs are dominated by dollar moves; crosses isolate the relative view between two other economies.

### The Cross Pair Tiebreaker {cross-pair-tiebreaker}
- When both currencies in a view are moving against USD, the cross shows which is stronger.

### Trading a Fundamental View With a Cross {how-to-trade-fundamental-view-with-a-cross-pair}
- Pair the strongest currency against the weakest to express a cleaner thesis.

### Choosing Your Crosses {choosing-your-crosses}
- Mind spreads, liquidity and volatility; crosses usually cost more to trade.

## Carry Trade {carry-trade}

### What is a Carry Trade? {what-is-carry-trade}
- Borrow a low yielding currency to buy a high yielding one and earn the rate differential.

### How Carry Trades Work {how_do_carry_trades_work_for_forex}
- Positive swap accrues daily (rollover); leverage magnifies both carry and price risk.

### To Carry or Not to Carry {to-carry-or-not-to-carry}
- Works best in calm, risk-on markets with stable or widening rate gaps.

### Carry Trade Case Study: USD/JPY {carry-trade-case-study-usdjpy}
- Wide US Japan rate gap supported long USD/JPY; reversals were sharp when the gap narrowed or risk aversion hit.

### Carry Trade Criteria and Risk {carry-trade-criteria-and-risk}
- Look for a wide, stable differential, low volatility and supportive trend.

### Carry Trade Unwind {carry-trade-unwind}
- Crowded carry exits in panic create fast, deep reversals.

### Carry Trade Risk Management {carry-trade-risk-management}
- Smaller size, stops, diversification and watching volatility and rate expectations.

### Summary: Carry Trade {summary-carry-trade}
- Earns steady income in calm markets, suffers in sudden risk-off.

## Trading the News {trading-the-news}

### The Importance of News {importance-of-news}
- News shifts expectations and creates the biggest short term moves.

### Why Trade the News? {why-trade-the-news}
- Volatility and clear catalysts, at the cost of spreads and slippage.

### News Literacy {news-literarcy}
- Distinguish headline from detail, first print from revision, and fact from interpretation.

### Which Reports Are Trade Worthy? {which-reports-are-trade-worthy}
- Focus on what the central bank is watching right now.

### What Has the Market Priced In? {what-has-market-priced-in}
- Use rate futures and consensus to judge expectations; only surprises move price.

### Central Bank Communication {central-bank-communication}
- Statements, minutes, speeches and press conferences often matter more than the decision itself.

### Off Calendar Risk {off-calendar-risk}
- Unscheduled events (geopolitics, leaks, interventions) can hit at any time.

### Dangers of News Trading {dangers-of-news-trading}
- Spread widening, slippage, whipsaws and gaps through stops.

### Pre-Positioning {pre-positioning}
- Taking a position before a release based on a view; higher risk if the surprise goes the other way.

### Trading After the Initial Reaction {trading-after-the-initial-reaction}
- Let the first spike settle, then trade the direction that holds.

### Straddle Trade {straddle-trade}
- Buy stop above and sell stop below the pre-news range to catch the breakout either way; vulnerable to whipsaw.

### How Long Does a Catalyst Last? {how-long-does-a-catalyst-last}
- Some data moves fade in minutes; regime shifts in policy can drive trends for months.

### Reassessing the Market Environment {how-often-should-you-reassess-the-market-environment}
- Review your bias after major events and on a regular schedule.

### The Revision Problem {revision-problem}
- Data gets revised; prior month revisions can flip the read of a release.

### News Trading Toolkit {news-trading-toolkit}
- Calendar, consensus, rate pricing, squawk or newsfeed, and a written plan.

## Market Sentiment {market-sentiment}

### What is Market Sentiment? {what-is-market-sentiment}
- The prevailing attitude of traders; positioning can matter as much as fundamentals.

### The Contrarian Principle {contrarian-principle}
- When everyone is positioned one way, few are left to push further; extremes can reverse.

### Commitment of Traders Report {commitment-of-traders-report}
- Weekly CFTC report of futures positioning by commercials, non-commercials (speculators) and small traders.

### The COT Trading Strategy {the-cot-trading-strategy}
- Watch for extreme net speculative positioning as a reversal warning, not a timing tool.

### COT in Action {cot-in-action}
- Example of combining COT extremes with price signals.

### Retail Sentiment {retail-sentiment}
- Broker data on retail positioning; retail crowds are often wrong at turning points.

### Reading Retail Sentiment {reading-retail-sentiment}
- Use as a contrarian filter, mainly at extremes.

### Fear and Greed Index {fear-and-greed-index}
- Composite gauge of equity market mood; extremes hint at risk appetite turning.

### VIX: What It Tells Forex Traders {vix-what-it-tells-forex-traders}
- S&P 500 implied volatility; rising VIX signals risk-off, favouring JPY, CHF and USD.

### MOVE Index {move-index}
- US Treasury implied volatility; bond market stress spills into FX.

### CVIX {cvix}
- Currency implied volatility index; high readings mean bigger FX swings.

### Risk On, Risk Off {risk-on-risk-off-how-global-mood-moves-currencies}
- Risk-on lifts AUD, NZD, CAD and EM; risk-off lifts JPY, CHF and USD.

### Sentiment Confluence {sentiment-confluence}
- Line up positioning, volatility and risk mood with your technical and fundamental view.

### Summary: Market Sentiment {summary-of-market-sentiment}
- Sentiment explains why price can ignore fundamentals and when crowded trades snap back.
